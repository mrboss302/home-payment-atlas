Why a lower home price may not mean a lower monthly payment

A mortgage payment depends on the amount financed and the interest rate, but ongoing costs also include property taxes, insurance, PMI, and possibly HOA fees. Our comparison applies the same rate and down payment to each state while allowing the approximate property-tax assumption to differ.

For example, at a hypothetical 6.5% fixed rate, a $400,000 home with 20% down leaves a $320,000 loan. Principal and interest alone are about $2,023 a month over 30 years. At an illustrative 1.05% property tax and 0.35% insurance, the estimated total is about $2,489 per month, before HOA dues. These figures are calculated examples, not current lender rates or local quotations.

When you compare a different home-value tier, the underlying price changes. This is why you should compare similar property types and check actual listings and county tax bills before drawing conclusions.

How to use this comparison responsibly

  1. Start with two states and choose the same home type in both. The middle-tier Zillow estimate is not the median listing price.
  2. Use a rate offered by your own lender, not the default assumption. Try a rate one point higher to see the sensitivity.
  3. Check estimated upfront cash and the 28% housing-income guideline. Neither is a lending qualification decision.
  4. Follow each state's page for additional price tiers. Research your target county, neighborhood, insurance quote, and HOA before making a purchase decision.
